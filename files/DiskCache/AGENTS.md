# files/DiskCache

A bounded disk cache over PINCache. It depends on `third_party/PINCache` and on `core/Common`. It holds one category, `PINCache (VibeAudioCache)`, and a store over the system's SQLite, `VibeCacheStore`. It came from Vibe. `media/Metadata`'s cache keeps its tags and art in one `VibeCacheStore`, and `waveform/WaveformLoading`'s cache keeps its entries in another. The category also removes the stores other versions left.

## The store

**`audioCacheWithName:rootPath:` makes a store with the default byte and age limits**, `VibeAudioCacheDefaultByteLimit` and `VibeAudioCacheDefaultAgeLimit`. Each store may hold 1 GiB on disk. An entry nobody used for 180 days ages out, since a changed file identity can orphan entries and nothing else would remove them. **The 180 days count from an entry's last use, never from its write.** A hit re-dates its file once a day at most, and a write makes the entry anew. So an entry read each week stays, and one nobody reads still ages out. Until 2026-11-19 the days counted from the write, so art shown every day still went. A nil root puts the store under the user's caches, and a root of the caller's puts it there instead, as a test does.

**An app sets each store's limits through the piece that keeps it**: `AudioWaveformCache`'s `setByteLimit:ageLimit:`, and `AudioTrackMetadataCache`'s `setByteLimit:ageLimit:forStore:`. Each applies them on the piece's queue once the store is built: the waveform cache as `byteLimit` and `ageLimit` on its `diskCache`, and the metadata cache as the sum of its three budgets on its `VibeCacheStore` (`media/Metadata`'s AGENTS.md). An app that sets nothing keeps the defaults, which is what Vibe does. **A limit is set at once**, a fix this repo carries (`third_party/PINCache`'s AGENTS.md), so the latest limit wins, and each trim cuts to the limit as it stands when the trim runs. Before, PINDiskCache set each limit on its concurrent queue, so an app's limit could lose to the default set when the store was made, and the default's trim could cut a store the app gave more room.

**A store keeps no record of its entries in memory.** `audioCacheWithName:rootPath:` sets PINDiskCache's `recordsOnDemand`, a fix this repo carries (`third_party/PINCache`'s AGENTS.md). The byte limit lets a store hold about 250,000 entries, and PINDiskCache's record of each, about 170 bytes with its key, would hold about 40 MB for the app's life. Measured on an M4 Max over a store of 200,000 entries opened as at a launch, with a read each millisecond: 3.4 to 3.5 MB of footprint at idle on demand, against 44.7 to 44.8 MB for upstream's records, a peak of 3.5 MB against 294 MB, and a worst call of 5 ms against 0.55 s, as upstream's count holds the lock while it lists. The count is known at 1.1 s against 1.2 s. A hit takes 44 µs on demand, and 31 to 53 µs with records. A miss tries the file, 11 µs, where a record answered in 0.7 µs. A sweep lists the directory outside the store's lock, 1.6 s at 200,000 entries: a trim that has bytes to free, the age limit's trim when an entry may be past it, and each usage count. Only background work writes to a store.

**A write, a removal, and a move never wait for the launch's count.** Each first settles the keys it changes: the count skips a file a change already accounted for, and a change adds the bytes of a file the count has not reached. So the byte count, the byte limit's trim, the age limit's bound, and `audioDiskUsageWithCompletion:` come out as a fresh count would. A removal of everything during the count stops it. Until 2026-11-19 each waited for the count, about 2.1 s at 250,000 entries on a quiet Mac and 10 to 13 s under load, measured in another app: a new file's tags took 10 to 12 s to answer after a launch, and a one-song tag edit 2 to 12 s. Measured here on 2026-11-19 on an M4 Max at a load of about 4, over a store of 250,000 entries of 4 KB on disk, opened again with a write 50 ms later, three runs each: the write took 0.89 to 0.94 s before, the rest of the count, and 0.4 to 0.7 ms after, and a removal after it 0.2 to 0.3 ms. The byte count came out exact, 1,024,000,000, in both. The sweeps still wait for the count, since each needs the whole byte count. `third_party/PINCache`'s AGENTS.md has how the count keeps what it has accounted for. Open to change: it keeps 64-bit hashes of the file names, 4 MB up to 262,144 files and 8 MB past that, with 12 MB for a moment while it grows, not the names, about 25 MB. So a collision, under once in 10^8 launches, leaves one file's bytes uncounted until the next launch.

**Two limits are known and unchanged.** Two stores on one folder each keep their own byte count. A change through one, after the other's count listed the file, leaves the other's total wrong until its next launch. And keys that differ only in case would count twice on a case-insensitive volume. Every store here is keyed by `NSURL (Hash)`'s disk key, of digits, dashes, and lowercase hex, so none do.

**A store's reads take no lock, and its sweeps hold it little.** A hit and a miss take no lock, and a hit opens its file once, with no second stat for its dates. So no sweep or write stalls a read. A trim sorts its listing before it takes the lock, and a usage count holds no lock at all, so neither stalls a write or a removal for long. Measured on 2026-11-05 on an M4 Max at a load of about 240, over a store of 200,000 entries, with a read each millisecond: a hit retires 15% fewer instructions, 296,000 to 373,000 against 381,000 to 399,000, and a miss half, 56,000 against 112,000 to 134,000. A read's worst call during a trim was 20 ms, against 0.19 to 0.22 s, and during a usage count 25 to 54 ms, against 6.1 to 6.4 s. No writer was measured during either.

**A store's user reads and writes `diskCache` directly, never the memory cache.** On macOS `PINMemoryCache` never evicts. Its cost limit needs a cost for each entry, a disk hit fills it again at cost 0, and its memory-pressure hooks are iOS only. So it would pin every entry ever loaded for the app's life.

**`audioDiskUsageWithCompletion:` counts the entries and their bytes on disk.** The enumeration blocks, but holds no lock, so call it on the store's own serial queue. The completion is dispatched to the main thread.

The keys are the caller's. Every store is keyed by `core/Common`'s disk key, `NSURL (Hash)`. `audioDiskUsageOfCaches:completion:` sums the counts over several stores.

## Other versions' stores

**`removeOtherAudioCacheVersionsKeeping:rootPath:completion:` removes the stores other versions left.** A store's name carries its entry format's version, as `Audio Track Metadata v10` and `audio_waveform_cache_v7` do. A format change renames the store, so the old one is never opened again. Only an open store trims itself, so an old store would keep up to 1 GiB on disk for good. The rule removes a store when its name is a current name's apart from the version suffix, a space or an underscore then `v` and digits, and it is none of the current names. So two current names of one family both stay, as `media/Metadata`'s new store, `Audio Track Metadata`, and `Audio Track Metadata v11`, the store it imports, do. Before 2026-11-19 each kept name removed the other. A name with no suffix is a version too. Another store, and a directory that is no store, stay. An app calls it once at launch, with the name of every store it opens, and it runs off the calling thread. A version bump then needs no edit in the app. The waveform cache's `cacheName` is now `audio_waveforms`, of another family, so the rule removes none of the waveform stores, and the waveform cache's import removes them itself (`waveform/WaveformLoading/AGENTS.md`).

**The cost is a downgrade's.** An older build keeps only the names it knows. So it removes a newer build's store in the same way. Each switch between the two builds then costs one parse or decode of every file.

## Tests

The tests of the store's limits and usage are new here: the defaults at once, the latest limit winning, an entry read since its write that stays, and a hit before a relaunch that keeps an old entry while the one nobody read goes.

So are the tests of its records on demand: none kept, the byte count through writes, replacements, removals, and a reopening, a trim by use and one by date, and the records a plain store drops. The tests of changes during the launch's count hold the count through PINDiskCache's seam, before it lists, at a file it listed, or after the last. They check that a write, a removal, a move, and a hit answer while it is held, and they cover a key written or removed before the count reaches it, one removed after, a trim fired during the count, an old entry moved during it that still ages out, moves as Metadata makes them, two stores on one folder, and a removal of everything. Each ends with a byte count equal to the files' bytes and to a fresh store's count. They root a store in a temporary directory, check the limits, and read the usage back on main. The tests of the removal came from Vibe. They lay out every name a release shipped, keep the current stores, another store, and a directory that is no store, and remove the rest. A missing directory is a no-op. `waveform/WaveformLoading`'s tests and `media/Metadata`'s each check that their store's name follows the rule. The minimal consumer makes a store under a temporary root, writes an entry, and reads the usage back, as an app would.

## The SQLite store

**`VibeCacheStore` is a bounded store over the system's SQLite, for the caches to move onto.** `waveform/WaveformLoading` keeps its waveforms in one, with no shared items. `media/Metadata` keeps its tags and art in another, under its folder `Audio Track Metadata`, outside PINDiskCache's prefix, so the removal above never reaches it. Each entry is a row keyed by the caller's key, with a blob, the number of its format, the name of a shared item it points into, its size, its last use as a day, and when it was written. **The row's id is the first 8 bytes of the key's SHA-256, and the key is kept in the row**, which every read and change checks. So a lookup is one descent of the table's tree, with no index of the keys to read first, which halves the pages a first read from the disk takes. Two keys of one id replace each other, as a cache may, once in about 10^9 stores of 200,000 entries. The pages are 16 KB, which makes the trees shallower and packs a 1 KB row with less waste. A blob over `inlineLimit`, 64 KiB by default, goes to a file in `Files/` beside the database, and the row lists it. So a count or a trim reads rows, never the folder.

**Formats.** Every row carries its format's number, so a format change never empties a store. `hitForKey:format:` answers only a row of that format. `hitForKey:acceptingFormatsUpTo:` also answers an older row, and the hit says it is older. A newer row is a miss for both, so a downgrade never misreads one.

**Shared items** are a second table, keyed by a name and a part, that an entry names by its name, as a file's entry would name its picture's hash. Two entries that name one item share one copy. An item goes when its last entry goes or names another, through a trigger, or when a trim takes it. `removeSharedItemForName:part:` removes one part, as a trim may, and the entries that name it keep their rows. `setData:forKey:format:sharedName:missingSharedParts:partFormat:` writes an entry and the parts its item lacks in one transaction, so a parse's write commits once. Then the entries that named it read as naming an item that is not cached. The daily work removes an item that no entry ever named.

**Limits.** The creator sets `byteLimit` and `ageLimit`. 0 means none, and the defaults are 1 GiB and 180 days. The bytes are the blobs' and files' sizes, summed. A write over the byte limit starts a trim to 90% of it, least recently used first, entries and shared items together. The age counts from the last use. A read records its use once a day at most, and the uses go to the database together, half a second later. Ties within a day go by the order of writing, by each row's `written` time, since the ids are hashes. **One shared part may have a limit of its own** (`setByteLimit:ofSharedPart:`), as a cache keeps its large art apart from its entries. That part's rows count against it and never against `byteLimit`, a table the triggers keep holds each part's bytes, and its trim takes only that part's rows. So the part never pushes out an entry, and the entries never push out the part. A trim checks the limits again when it ends, so the bytes written during it are trimmed too. The age limit runs in the daily work, ten seconds after an open and then once a day. Each trim or sweep runs 64 rows a transaction, so a write waits for one batch at most.

**The usage is a sum the triggers keep.** `usage` reads one row, so nothing is counted at an open, and it never waits for a write.

**Threads.** Every method may be called from any thread. Writes, removals, and moves run on the store's serial queue and return once they read back. Reads borrow one of a few read connections and never wait for the writer, since the database uses WAL. Each connection's page cache is capped, 256 KiB for a reader and 1 MiB for the writer. A checkpoint runs on the queue, never inside a caller's write. The open prepares the write statements and makes the journal on the queue, so the first write doesn't pay for them.

**One open at a time.** The store holds an exclusive `flock` on `Cache.lock`. A second open, in this process or another, gets nil until the first closes. `close` waits for queued work, then frees the directory. A closed store reads as empty and ignores writes.

**Damage and crashes.** A database that will not open, or that a read or a write finds damaged, is removed with its files and made again, since a cache holds nothing the user made. A file being written is listed in `pending` before it is written, and leaves the list in the transaction that writes its row. A file that a removal, a replacement, or a trim lets go is listed by a trigger in that transaction, and removed once it commits. An open removes every listed file. So a crash at any point leaves no entry that reads as good and no file that is never removed. A file whose size is not its row's reads as a miss, and its row goes. `removeAllWithCompletion:` makes the database again, and removes the old files off the queue.

**An import lists another store's files** (`adoptFileAtPath:forKey:format:lastUse:`), for a cache that moves off PINCache. A file over `inlineLimit` is renamed into `Files/`, and a smaller one is read into its row and then removed. The new name is listed in `pending` before the rename, so a crash after it leaves a file the next open removes, and that one entry is lost. `lastUse` dates the row, so the limits see the entry's age as the old store recorded it. A key that already holds an entry keeps it, and the file is left for the caller. A rename that fails, as across volumes, answers NO. At an import's end, `removeFolderAtPath:` moves the old store's folder into the store's directory, under the name a removed `Files/` takes, so the same sweep at an open removes it if a quit comes first.

**A file is read into a buffer that is not zeroed first.** Zeroing a waveform's 215 KB took about a tenth of its hit's bytes. A map was tried, and was slower: 50 µs against 41 µs for a 215 KB entry, decoded.

**Measured** on 2026-11-19 on an M4 Max, at a load of 5 to 12, beside PINCache's store with records on demand, built the same way with `-O2` into one tool, three runs each. Each store held 200,000 entries of 200 bytes, as above, kept as a keyed archive, 457 bytes in the store. A hit decodes the archive in both.

Run again, they were 7 µs, 10 µs, and 0.21 ms. `media/Metadata`'s AGENTS.md has the store measured under the metadata cache, a cold run of first hits included.
